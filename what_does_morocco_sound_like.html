The project

A place imagined through sound.

What Does Morocco Sound Like? is an exploratory project about translating impressions of place into generative audio. Instead of reconstructing a documentary soundscape, it uses synthesis, noise, slowly shifting tones and resonant textures to imagine different acoustic environments.

The sketches move between two kinds of space: the open, exposed landscape of the desert and the dense, intimate spaces of a medina or a blue-painted mountain town. Each patch offers a different listening perspective rather than a definitive portrait of Morocco.

These are artistic sound studies, not documentary recordings. Field recordings can become a next layer of the project.

Approach · EchoForma

Not a recording of a place, but an imagined space made audible.

The next stage can introduce selected field recordings from medinas, markets, water and traditional instruments. These materials would be treated as another layer in the composition, while preserving the distinction between recorded sound and artistic synthesis.
