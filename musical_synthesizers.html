The exhibition

Instruments as
living systems.

The project develops the architecture of a multimedia environment dedicated to the history and phenomenology of musical synthesizers. Rather than presenting sound as a fixed recording, it treats electronic sound as a process: continuous, variable and responsive to its surroundings.

01 / SOUND

Generative by nature

Code-based sound generation creates evolving material without relying on pre-prepared audio files.

02 / SPACE

Immersive environment

Sound and projection form a continuous landscape adapted to the architecture of each exhibition room.

03 / TIME

Always in motion

Modulation, filtering, noise and changing parameters make each moment distinct rather than repeating a fixed cycle.

System architecture

A connected
multimedia space.

The exhibition concept combines generative audio, real-time visual synthesis and a centralized control structure. Each room can develop its own sonic and visual identity while remaining part of one coordinated environment.

01 / Generative audio

Csound is the sound-generation environment. Each room can use its own system of oscillators, noise generators, spectral modulation and algorithmic processes.

  • Real-time sound generation
  • Amplitude variation and timbral filtering
  • Delay, feedback and spatial depth
  • Continuous material with high variability

02 / Real-time visuals

TouchDesigner creates visuals that respond to the nature and parameters of the sound rather than relying on pre-rendered video.

  • Waveforms and spectral clouds
  • Modulation structures and abstract interfaces
  • Sound-to-visual synchronization via OSC/UDP
  • Adaptation to the visual scenario of each room

03 / Spatial audio

The sound environment is designed to feel continuous and immersive, without drawing attention to isolated playback points.

  • Acoustic environment shaped around each room
  • Movement and depth controlled through parameters
  • Sound levels and timbre adapted to the space

04 / Integrated control

Audio, DSP, visualization and the AV control layer are conceived as components of a single coordinated system.

  • Parameter exchange between system components
  • Synchronous audio and visual operation
  • Centralized control of the multimedia environment

Not simply a history of instruments, but an invitation to experience electronic sound as a living, changing space.
